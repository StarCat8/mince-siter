Legacy spectroscopic survey

Neutron capture elements
at intermediate metallicity

The understanding of the physics of the different neutron capture processes (slow, rapid and possibly intermediate) and the comprehension of the Milky Way chemical evolution require precise abundances of n-capture elements in metal-poor stars. The intermediate metallicity range (−2.5 <[Fe/H]< −1.5) is particularly interesting because it allows discriminating different models. Surprisingly there are far fewer measurements of n-capture elements abundances in this metallicity range than at lower metallicities. The Measuring at Intermediate metallicity Neutron Capture Elements (MINCE) project aims at filling this gap. Thanks to several facilities spread all over the world, we will determine precise chemical abundances in metal-poor giants. The legacy of the MINCE project will be a catalogue of about 1,000 high quality stellar spectra with precise atmospheric parameters and chemical abundances.

What is MINCE

Mapping the heavy elements of the Galaxy

The Measuring at Intermediate metallicity Neutron Capture Elements project aims to fill a critical gap in our understanding of Galactic chemical evolution by providing a homogeneous catalog of ~1,000 metal-poor giants with precise abundances for 30+ elements.

⚛️

Science goals

Disentangle the s-, r- and i-process contributions to heavy element enrichment across −3 < [Fe/H] < −1.
