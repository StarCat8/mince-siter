Eight world-class
telescope facilities

MINCE observations use high-resolution echelle spectrographs mounted on telescopes across Europe, the Americas and beyond, covering both hemispheres.

Aperture sizes listed above are taken from config.js — edit that file to correct them. All instruments deliver R ≥ 50,000 with S/N ≥ 100 at 5000 Å per pixel. Wavelength coverage spans roughly 3800–9000 Å depending on the spectrograph, sufficient to measure lines of elements from Li to Pb.
Why so many telescopes?

Northern & southern sky coverage

Metal-poor stars are distributed across the whole sky. No single observatory can reach all targets. The multi-facility strategy also enables cross-checks of the analysis pipeline between instruments.

🌍

Full sky coverage

TNG, CFHT, OHP, NOT and Molėtai cover the northern hemisphere; VLT, MPG/ESO and Magellan cover the south. Together they reach every PASTEL target.

✅

Cross-calibration

Overlapping targets observed with multiple instruments allow systematic offsets between pipelines to be identified and corrected before combining results.

⏱️

Telescope time efficiency

Distributing targets across facilities means that bright targets go to smaller telescopes, freeing VLT and Magellan time for the faintest, most metal-poor stars.
