Scientific background

Neutron capture processes
in the metal-poor universe

Elements heavier than iron cannot be forged in ordinary stellar burning. They require an intense flux of neutrons — and their abundances encode the history of some of the most extreme events in the universe.

The three channels

s, r, and i — three neutron capture processes

Heavy elements from strontium (Z=38) to uranium (Z=92) are produced via neutron capture onto seed nuclei. The neutron density determines which of three regimes applies.

r-process · rapid

Neutron star mergers & rare CCSNe

At neutron densities nn ≫ 10²⁰ cm⁻³, the rapid process builds nuclei far from stability. The 2017 kilonova GW170817 confirmed NSMs as a major r-process site. Europium (Eu) is its best tracer.

s-process · slow

AGB stars

At low neutron densities (nn ~ 10⁷ cm⁻³), captures proceed near the valley of stability. AGB stars are the dominant site. At low metallicity [Fe/H] < −1.5, their contribution is minimal. Barium (Ba) is a key tracer.

i-process · intermediate

Super-AGB & rapidly accreting WDs

At intermediate densities (10¹³–10¹⁵ cm⁻³), the i-process may explain the abundance patterns of CEMP-s/r stars and certain metal-poor giants that cannot be reproduced by s or r alone.

Goals

What MINCE aims to answer

  • What is the relative contribution of NSMs vs CCSNe to the r-process budget?

    The scatter in [Eu/Fe] at low metallicity is a key diagnostic. A large homogeneous sample is needed to separate stochastic enrichment from systematic trends.

  • When does the s-process turn on?

    AGB stars have a minimum metallicity threshold. The [Ba/Eu] ratio tracks the s/r balance — MINCE provides the statistical power to map its evolution precisely.

  • What is the frequency and abundance signature of i-process events?

    Specific [La/Eu], [Ba/Sr] patterns distinguish the i-process from mixed s+r. A sample of 1,000 stars allows a statistical determination of its occurrence rate.

  • How did the Milky Way assemble its metal-poor halo?

    Chemical "tagging" with neutron capture elements can distinguish accreted dwarf-galaxy debris from in-situ halo stars, complementing kinematic studies.

Methodology

How we measure abundances

1

Target selection

Metal-poor giants (0 < log g < 2.5, −3 < [Fe/H] < −0.5, 4000 < Teff < 5500 K) selected from PASTEL and literature compilations.

2

High-resolution spectroscopy

Spectra at R ≥ 50,000 and S/N ≥ 100 per pixel at 5000 Å, obtained with 8 spectrographs. All data reduced with instrument-specific pipelines.

3

Atmospheric parameters

Teff from photometric calibrations and spectroscopic excitation balance; log g from ionisation equilibrium; [Fe/H] from Fe II lines; ξ from curve of growth.

4

Abundance analysis

1D LTE equivalent-width analysis and spectrum synthesis for blended lines. NLTE corrections applied where available. MARCS model atmospheres.

Sample selection criteria
